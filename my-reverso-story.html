The Genesis

Ninety-five years
of turning over.

A cracked watch glass on an Indian polo ground started it all. What began as a fix for a sporting problem became one of the most personal — and most technically ambitious — watches ever made.

1930 – 1931 · The Challenge

A Cracked Crystal in British India

Travelling through India over the winter of 1930–31, Swiss businessman César de Trey fell in with a set of British army officers for whom polo was a way of life — and whose watch glasses rarely survived a match. One of them, freshly out a crystal, put the challenge to him directly: build something that could take the hit. De Trey brought the problem home to Jacques-David LeCoultre, who called on French designer René-Alfred Chauvot to solve it. Chauvot's answer was a case that slid free of its cradle on two spring-loaded studs and span a full 180 degrees, hiding the crystal behind a blank steel back. The patent — for "a watch capable of sliding in its support and being completely turned over" — was filed on the 4th of March, 1931. They named it Reverso, Latin for "I turn around."

1936 · The Canvas Is Discovered

The First Enamelled Case-Back

The reverse plate was only ever engineered to survive impact — an anonymous slab of steel. Owners saw something else in it. The earliest documented enamelled Reverso dates to 1936, a yellow-gold case decorated with a portrait scene, and it marks the moment the "protective" back quietly became the more personal of the watch's two faces. Monograms, crests, and miniature enamels followed within a few years, well before the idea had a name.

1937 · One House, One Name

Jaeger and LeCoultre Formally Merge

Edmond Jaeger's Paris workshop and the LeCoultre manufacture in Le Sentier had already been supplying each other for decades — the Reverso's case had come from exactly this partnership. In 1937 the two firms formally merged into a single maison, Jaeger-LeCoultre, giving the watch that made the union necessary a permanent home.

1991 · The 60th Anniversary

The First Complications Arrive

For its 60th birthday, the Reverso grew up: a larger "Grande Taille" case, a display case-back, and — for the first time in the line's history — genuine complications on the dial side, starting with a power-reserve indicator and a date window. The limited "60ème" model is now considered the watch that turned a single design classic into an entire collection.

Today

Still Turning

Nearly a century on, the Reverso has stayed in continuous production, carried dozens of different calibres, and remained watchmaking's clearest example of one mechanical idea becoming an entire identity. In 2025, Jaeger-LeCoultre revisited its own origin story with the "1931 Polo Club" theme — and every case still leaves the workshop with a blank back, which means, by design, no two ever have to end up the same.

War, silence, a forgotten box of cases, a watch with four faces, and countless stories — there's a lot more between 1931 and today.
